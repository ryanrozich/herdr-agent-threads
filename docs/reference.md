# Agent Threads reference

The long reference for [RECIPE.md](../RECIPE.md): every surface the pane draws, and how each agent other than Claude Code is found and read. It lives here so RECIPE.md stays under the 64 KB that the herdr.dev/mods index reads.

## What you see

- **Floating top** (`floatTop`, the list and the outline; there is no header row): a 32 px band (`TOP_BAND`) over the list, painted as a fade from the list's colour to clear (`fadeLayer`: a gradient with `window.paint_path` on Herdr 0.1.15, four steps of alpha before that). Top left, always shown, the state (`floatState`): a 7 px dot in the state's colour and its word in 12 px, at every width, or what the pane is doing before it has one ("Reading… 42%", "Finding…", "No session"); its tooltip names the state, "click to go to the terminal (t)", the session's title and the binding, and a click goes to the terminal as `t` does. Top right, only while the pointer is in the pane (`paneHover`) or the pane has the keyboard, settings or a search is open: Search, Pinned (a pin with the pin count at its corner) and Settings, each on a patch in the list's colour (`floatButtons`). While you scroll, the day pill floats at the top centre and goes 1.5 s after the last scroll (`DAY_PILL_MS`). The list starts 32 px down (a `gap` row), and settings, the search field and the banners open in the flow under the band (`topPanels`). The agent, the turn count, the hidden count and the title are not shown with the floating top: the settings card's foot held them (`aboutRow`) until the version line took that row, and their next place is not chosen.
- **Header** (the Pinned view and the Pinned pane only; 40 px, `border` line below): a Claude badge (the Claude mark on a 14 px `#d97757` tile, then "Claude" in 12 px from 300 px; for another agent a neutral chip with its name and "Not readable", for a remote terminal a chip with the machine name and "Not read yet"). The badge's tooltip names the binding: "Reading t4 · session 7f3a1b2c · agent shop-agent · 1.2M tokens · cache 97.1% (read … · written … · new input …) · turn #14: cache 95.9% (read … · written … · new input …)", the agent part only when the session records an agent name (replies carry none), the last part for the selected turn, so a pane bound to the wrong terminal or session shows without a click. Then "N turns · M hidden · <session title>" in `muted` 12.5 px (N counts the turns shown; "· M hidden" counts the agent messages hidden in the whole list, every session, the same number as the Agents button's corner count and tooltip, and shows from 300 px; "· guessed" when the session was guessed from the folder, "Reading… 42%" while a transcript is read, "No session" when none was found). Right side, 24 px icon buttons, `dim` at rest, `hover` background: Filter (`/`), Agents (a bot icon, `a`; only when the session has agent messages or the setting is on, and from 300 px; tooltip "Show agent messages (40 folded, 1 hidden)" or "Hide agent messages", a part left out when it is 0; a corner count shows how many are folded or hidden, or shown when on), Pin (`p`, from 300 px, only in the Pinned view: the list's header leaves pinning to `p` and an open turn's pin, so the text keeps that room), then a labelled button, "Pinned 2" (the pin count; 12 px `muted` text in a 24 px `border` box, `accent` border and `raised` fill while the Pinned column is open or the pane shows the Pinned view, when it reads "All turns"), Settings (sliders icon, `,`). From 440 px the agent's state follows the badge: a 7 px dot in the state's colour and its word in 12 px, in a 24 px box with 5 px padding a side and the `hover` fill on hover, a pointer cursor and a click that focuses the terminal as `t` does (its tooltip ends "click to go to the terminal (t)"). Under 440 px the badge drops its word and the state becomes an 8 px dot on the badge's corner; the badge then takes the click, the same `hover` fill and the tooltip's click line, since the dot is too small to aim at. `headerButtonWidth` in `ui.js` sizes the buttons for `headerLayout`. The Pinned view's header leads with a 14 px `accent` pin and "Pinned" in 13.5 px semibold `foreground`, then (in the same pane only) the badge, then "2 of 5" in `muted`. It has a 2 px `accent` bar along its top and `accent` at 8% behind it, so the column reads as its own view beside the list. Herdr titles every copy of a mod's pane with the mod's name and gives a mod no way to rename its pane, so the heading carries the name. The Pinned pane's header has no badge, then the pin button (from 300 px) and "All turns".
- **Settings card** (`,` or the sliders button), a card over the list, under the icons at the top right (`settingsCard`): at most 440 px wide (the pane less 12 px each side when narrower), as tall as its rows up to its room, its rows scrolling inside it (their height is measured, `cardBody`); the list does not move. Its room ends at least `CARD_STRIP` (160 px) above the pane's bottom, so a strip of the list stays in view under it, or above the key hints when they are taller; in a pane under `CARD_STRIP_MIN` (514 px) the strip shrinks a pixel for each pixel the pane loses, down to 12 px above the bottom or the key hints, so shrinking the pane never grows the card. Its room is at least 180 px, over the key hints if it must, but it never ends lower than 12 px above the pane's bottom: in a very short pane the rows shrink first, to nothing if need be, and in one too short for the head and foot under the icons (under 126 px) the card moves up over the icons, so its head and foot always show. There it covers the sliders button; its x, Esc, `,` and a press elsewhere in the pane still close it. Keys while it is open and no field has the keyboard (`cardKey`): ↑/↓ and j/k a line (`CARD_LINE`, 40 px), Page Up/Page Down/Space a page (the view less a line), Home/End the top or the end; the list does not move. Herdr can only bring a scroll area's direct child into view, with the smallest scroll (`ScrollHandle.scroll_to_item`), so after the rows the scroll area holds a 1 px mark every `CARD_MARK` (8) px and one on the last pixel (`cardMarks`); `cardScroll` picks the mark that puts the top where the key wants it (going down, a mark a view's height under the new top, brought up to the bottom edge; going up, the mark at the new top). The card keeps where it is per page (`cardPlace`: y, moved by the keys and by the wheel's `delta.y`, heard on the rows and the area and counted once), and one handle per page (`cardScrolls`, made in init); an opening asks each for its mark at 0, so the card opens at the top. No ScrollHandle (Herdr before 0.1.15): no marks, and those keys do nothing on the card. The scrollbar is drawn all the time (`Scrollbar.vertical(id).mode("always")` in a `CARD_BAR_W` 10 px strip at the right edge, paired by id with an `overflow_y_scroll` area) where Herdr has `Scrollbar` and `mode` (`"mode" in div()`); otherwise the area is `overflow_y_scrollbar`, whose bar shows while it scrolls. The keys sheet's two pieces: a clear layer over the pane that closes it on a press anywhere else in the pane (the press does nothing else, no turn is selected), and the card, which stops the press; `on_mouse_down_out` on the card closes it on a press in another pane, if Herdr delivers one. While it or the keys sheet is open nothing under it takes a hover, press or wheel (`covered`: the kit cannot block hover under a layer, so the rail, the list and the rows check it; the clear layer stops the wheel). No scrim; `shadow_2xl`, as the kit has no custom shadow. Spacing per the settings-spacing design: a 16 px inset on every band (12 under 320 px), 40 px head and foot, rows at least 28 px, groups 20 px apart, readable text `muted` at the faintest. It also closes with Esc (one press from any control, the quote key field included), the x at its top right ("Close (Esc)"), the sliders button or `,`; opening it gives the pane the keyboard. Its title "Settings" (the `,` key is in the sliders button's tooltip), then one page that scrolls (its sections and their rows are one list, `CARD_SECTIONS`; how far above the pane's bottom it ends at the least is `CARD_STRIP`, 160, so a strip of the list stays in view; under `CARD_STRIP_MIN`, 514 px, the strip shrinks with the pane), opening at the top (its scroll area's id counts the openings, `cardOpens`, as Herdr keeps a scroll position by id). No tabs and no preview: a change shows in the list behind the card. Its sections, in order (`CARD_SECTIONS`, the owner's order B): **Messages** (Text size [−] 14 [+], 12 to 18; User message color, a row of 24 px dots (under the name when they do not fit beside it), `ui.js` BUBBLES plus the file's own `#rrggbb` when it holds one, the chosen one ringed in `accent`, its name the dot's tooltip, "Blue · default"; Shorten long messages, `truncate`, and only while it is on Prompt lines [−] 4 [+] and Reply lines [−] 6 [+], indented under it; Always show time, `alwaysShowTime`); **Agent messages** (Show agent messages, with "25 hidden" or "40 folded · 1 hidden" beside its switch, no toast from here; Agent messages on the rail, `agentRail`, off by default; Agent message color, the same dots, "Amber · default"; the chat's bubbles use both); **Layout** (Rail side, Left and Right, each with its icon, `panel-left` and `panel-right`; Key hints at the bottom, `keyHints`, off by default); **Links and keys** (Link rules with the count and "N not working", which opens the Link rules page, and Quote key, a one-letter field: a free letter a to z is the quote key at once and is written as `quoteKey`; a letter Threads uses, an empty field or anything else leaves the key and says so under the field, "j is a Threads key already. The quote key stays q."); **File** (the path, cut at a slash from its start, `ui.js` cutPath, and Copy path; no Open, since `cx.open_url` takes only http and https addresses and the mod runs no file opener, which says "Copied" in its own place for 2 s, with no toast; a file that is not valid JSON says the defaults apply). The version line, `versionRow`, is the card's foot's first item (below): "Agent Threads 1.0.0" from `version.js` VERSION, with the commit from `build.js` BUILD, which `./install.sh` writes in the installed copy from `git describe --tags --long --dirty` (`version.js` versionLine: "1.0.0, commit: abc1234", ", with local changes"; the tooltip adds "3 commits after the 1.0.0 release"; null, as in the repo or a copy made without install.sh, reads "(commit not known)" with a tooltip saying why), 12 px `muted`, plain text and not a control, truncated in a narrow card, where the tooltip of a known commit is the whole line, with the commit count when there is one. Rows have no icons and the switches no hint lines. The page ends with Reset to defaults, once (every control on the card: text size, both colors, agent messages, agent messages on the rail, Shorten long messages and its lines, the time, rail side and key hints; faded while every one is at its default) and, under it, "Link rules and the quote key stay." At the card's foot, outside the scroll: the version line, and "? keys", which opens the keys sheet. A change from the card shows no toast. Keys on the card (`cardStop`, `moveRing`): Tab and Shift-Tab move a ring through every control in the order drawn, round the ends (Close, or Back and Close on a rules page; then each stepper button, colour dot, switch and choice, Sender rules, Link rules, the quote key field, Copy path, Reset to defaults and "? keys"; on Link rules and Sender rules each rule's Edit, Delete and switch, Undo, Use, Add rule, and an open rule's types or Change, its fields, Reference, Open, Keep rule, Discard, Move up, Duplicate and Delete), and Enter or Space presses the control with the ring as a click does (`ringKey`). Herdr's window takes plain Tab for itself before a pane's `on_key_down` sees it, so the mod binds Tab and Shift-Tab once for all its panes (`cardKeys`, `ui.js` keyInstaller: by the first pane's `init`, whatever Herdr answers, and, only if that call threw, once more at the card's first opening, a try no other pane's `init` can spend; never from render) to the actions `agent-threads.ringNext` and `agent-threads.ringBack` in the key context `ThreadsCard`, which the pane's root declares while the card is open (`cardKeyContext`); its `on_action` handlers move the ring as the keys do. The card's text fields are not stops in the window's Tab order (`tab_stop(false)`). The debug report says what installing the keys answered (`settingsCard.keys`) and where the keyboard is (`keyboard`: `pane`, `field:<name>` or `elsewhere`). It also says where the card's view is on its page (`settingsCard.view`: `y` and `h`) and where the control with the ring is in the rows (`settingsCard.ringAt`: top and bottom, or null in the head or foot). Without a ring, Space scrolls the card as above. The ring is 2 px of the theme's accent, 3 px outside the control and round its corners; a text field shows it with its own accent edge and takes the keyboard while the ring is on it. The pane keeps the keyboard otherwise, so Esc and the panel's keys work whatever the ring is on, and closing the card leaves the keyboard in the pane. A press that takes its control away moves the ring: Link rules or Sender rules to Back, and Back to the row that opened the page; Edit into the rule (its first control), and Esc or Discard back to where it opened from; Delete to Undo; Undo and Keep rule to the rule's Edit; a Keep rule that would not run to the field that says why. Anything else that goes takes the ring to the control that now follows where it was. When the ring moves to a control the card's view does not show in full, the card scrolls just far enough to show it (`revealRing`, `ui.js` cardReveal: a control above comes to the top edge, one below to the bottom edge, by the same 8 px marks the keys use). The rows are built as columns (`cardCol`: each group, the rules list, an open rule and its types, the examples, and the line steppers under Shorten long messages; a row with parts under it returns them as `CardParts`, each a part of its own), so a control's place is known from the heights of what comes before it (`ringSpan`). A column's height is the sum of its parts (`cardColH`); every other part's height is measured as it draws and kept by the part, not by its place (`cardSizes`: its kid's place in the list it was built from, its place in what that kid returned, and the first control it holds), so a row that comes or goes leaves the others right. A new part not measured yet takes the height last measured in its place (`cardSlots`), as Herdr may not report a height that did not change. The scroll is worked out only once the rows' measured height fits the sum of their parts, or the rows were measured since they last changed; the head and foot always show. When the rows get shorter (a rule closes), the card's place is kept inside them (at most the rows' height less the view's), so the report and the next scroll start from where the card really is. A scroll waits for the heights it needs for 250 ms at the most (`REVEAL_WAIT`); the wheel, the scroll keys, another page and closing the card drop it. Nothing shows when the ring moves, and a pointer press on the card takes the ring away, so a click never shows one. Inside a coloured bubble the text is drawn with a theme whose text, muted, dim and accent colours are the bubble's ink (white or `#111111`, whichever reads better on the fill, `ui.js` bubbleInk), so quotes, a command's name and Show more read on it. TextView draws Markdown in the theme's text colour whatever its parent sets, so on a coloured bubble the open turn's message is plain text with quotes behind their bar.
- **Link rules page** (Link rules, in Links and keys): a back arrow and "Link rules", then "A link rule turns text in a reply into a link. A rule can make ACME-412 open its ticket.", then "Rules · N", each rule in the file in a framed box: its type's icon (`regex` for Custom, `ticket` for Ticket key, `git-pull-request` for Pull request), its name, Edit and Delete, then a switch (`on`; off keeps the rule, writes `"on": false`, dims the row and adds "Off"), its description when it has one, its example with the part it links underlined in `accent` and the address under it (or "No example yet" and its pattern, "Finds nothing in its example", or "This session has no repository, so this rule links nothing here."), and for a rule Threads will not run a warning mark in place of the switch, a frame tinted `blocked` and "Not working: <the reason>". Edit and Delete are 24 px icon buttons on every row (`ruleIcon`, `pencil.svg` and `trash.svg`), with the tooltips and spoken labels "Edit rule" and "Delete rule"; Delete turns `blocked` under the pointer. The switch sits last, in a 36 by 24 hit area, so the switches line up; a rule that is not working has its warning mark there. A press anywhere else on a row opens it, as Edit does. Delete takes the rule at once and leaves "Deleted <name>." with Undo where it was, until the page is left or another rule goes; Undo puts it back in its place. With two or more rules, "When two rules match the same text, the higher one wins.", then Add rule (up to 20 rules), then the file's path with Copy path. With no rules, "No rules yet." and the two types that fill a rule in dashed boxes under "Templates, not rules until you keep one", each with Use. **The open rule**, in place of its row (a new one under the list, headed "New rule, not kept yet"; one rule open at a time): Type, as three rows (Custom "Write it yourself.", Ticket key, Pull request, each with its icon and line, `settings.js` LINK_TEMPLATES) while a new rule's Finds, Opens and Example are untouched, where a press fills them (and the name, unless one was typed); after the first change to those fields, and on a kept rule, one line with the type and Change, which brings the rows back with "The label changes; the fields stay." Then Name, Description (80 characters at most, "It shows under the name in the list."), Finds (mono, with "A JavaScript regular expression. Capitals count. Reference", Reference underlined and opening MDN's regular expression cheat sheet with `cx.open_url`), Opens (mono, "An address that starts with https://. $1 puts in what the first ( ) found; {repo} is this session's repository.") and Example. Under them, what the rule does to its example, with the address and Open (`cx.open_url`), and, when a rule higher in the list takes the same text first, "<name> is higher in the list and takes this text first." with Move up. Keep rule (or Enter in a field), Discard (or Esc, from a field too: it closes the rule, not the card), and on a kept rule Move up, Duplicate (a new rule under it, "<name> copy", not kept until Keep rule) and Delete. **When a fault shows** (round 10): never at a key; when typing stops for 1.5 s (`REASON_PAUSE`, one `cx.timer.after` per key, a newer key voiding the older timer), when the field is left (the Input's blur) or on Keep rule, which keeps nothing while a fault stands and gives the first faulty field the keys. Once shown, a fault follows each key and goes the moment the rule runs. It sits under Finds or Opens ("Not working: <reason>", the field's edge in `blocked`); an empty field is a fault only on Keep rule; a kept rule that is not working opens with its reason showing. While the rule does not run, the result keeps the last one that did, at 45%. Every change writes the whole list as `links` at once (`writeLinks`: the page shows it before the save ends), with the rest of the file kept; keys a rule holds that this version does not know are kept. While the file does not read as settings, or holds a `links` that is not a list, the page is read only: no switch, no Add rule, and "The settings file does not read as settings now, so rules change only in the file until it is fixed."
- **Filter field** (shown while filtering): 30 px, `chrome` fill, `accent` border while typing, placeholder "Filter turns, or #12 to jump", a `/` key chip while empty, then the place among the matches across every session in the pane (in the Pinned view, among the pins), counted from the newest ("2 of 7 · newest first", "7 of 7 · first mention", "1 of 1 · only mention", "7 matches" while no match is selected, "No matches"; under 420 px the place alone, with "· first" at the oldest; "· 2 hidden" for matches among hidden agent messages), two step buttons (chevron up "Older match (n)", chevron down "Newer match (Shift n)", the one with no match left that way at 35% opacity with no pointer or hover, taking no press, its tooltip "No older match" or "No newer match"; no wrap), and a clear (×) button. A new search selects its newest match and centres it; new words keep the selection while it still matches. "#12" in the field does not filter: it selects turn 12 (of the newest session that has one) and scrolls to it, the counter says "turn 12" or "no turn 12", and Enter keeps the turn selected and closes the field. Matches are marked with a `working` tint (32%) in row previews and in the open turn's message, and stronger in the selected turn's rows: up to 60%, as far as the text on it still reads at 4.5:1 (`ui.js` matchFill, measured unrounded over the colour the match is drawn on, which each caller passes: the background for reply text, the selected fill over it for a selected outline line, and a bubble's fill over it for a message. On Herdr's light theme it is 60% everywhere. On its dark one it is 48% on reply text, 40% on a selected outline line, 42% in a plain bubble, and 32% in a blue bubble, where 32% is already under 4.5:1, as on main. A compact pin marks no matches).
- **Banners** under the header when they apply. Neutral blocks (`chrome` fill, `border` line): "Session ended · claude exited at 2:31 PM" or "Session ended · t4 closed at 2:31 PM", with "The N turns read so far stay here…" and Close pane; "t4 moved to tab "x"" with Terminal and Close. Tinted cards (the colour at 12%, border at 33%): Guessed session (`working`, with "Choose from N…" when there are several candidates), Could not read the transcript (`blocked`), Transcript not found (`blocked`: the file was deleted or moved; the turns read so far stay, and the banner goes when the file comes back).
- **Column**: the column steps with the pane between `ui.js` `STEPS`, 70, 90 and 120 characters a line; `readType(size, chars)` gives the line (`measure`, 461, 592 or 790 px at 14 px) and a bubble's line (`bubble`, never past 90 characters, `BUBBLE_CHARS`), and `fitWidth(size, chars)` the step's width, the line plus `GUTTER.MAX` each side (573, 704 or 902 px at 14 px). `columnStep(px, size, cur)` picks the step from the pane's px (its measured width plus the 2 px frame): up to the widest whose `fitWidth` it reaches, and down only when it is `STEP_BACK` (16 px, scaled with the text size) under the step shown, so 704 and 902 up, 688 and 886 down, at 14 px. `columnWidth(paneW, size, chars)` is the column the turns are laid out in: the step's line between side gutters of up to 56 px, which give way first, down to 24 px (`GUTTER`), after which the text follows the pane; plus the rows' own 16 px each side. A wider pane centres it (`colLeft`), so spare room is split evenly, and a resize inside a step moves only those margins: the layout keys (`sigBase`) hold the step (`chars()`), not the pane's width. `fitColumn` sets the width and the step (`colStep`) together, so the hold through a drag holds the step too: in a long chat (`rewrapCostly`) a change of width or step waits until the pane has been still for `ui.js` `REWRAP_PAUSE_MS` (1.2 s), and a resize inside a step changes neither, so nothing waits. Bubbles and replies end at the same edge of the column. `w` and the fit button (`fitToReading`) size the pane to its step's width (`readingPx`), followed by a weight that keeps it once it has applied and the pane has held still 300 ms (`sizeTo`, `weighSoon`, `toWeight`, `weighStep`; RECIPE.md, The pane's width is a weight). `Cmd-Shift-T` opens a pane at `readingPx` for the step its first width gives: the launcher marks the new pane in `shared("threads.fit")`, and the pane, once it has measured itself and read the settings, runs `layout.resize` to that width if it is wider, and clears the mark; a narrower one goes to a weight. Nothing else resizes the pane: there is no cap and no snap-back. `this.width` is the column, `pw()` the pane; the header, the Pinned column's width and the footer go by the pane, and presses on the list are read in the column's px (`colLeft()`). A settings file from before the steps may hold `width` and `readingWidth`: they are read with no error or note, and a save keeps them, as it keeps any key it does not know.
- **Wide tables**: a Markdown table in a reply (or a code block in a command's output) may run wider than its step's line, centred on it, out to the pane's edge less the column's gutter on each side and less the rail's lane (`tableExtra`): the room Full used to give it, in every step. Its wanted width is each column's longest cell, up to 40 characters, plus Herdr's cell padding (`ui.js` `tableNeed`). In an open turn a table is a list item of its own (`splitTables` on each Markdown run, `r.table`); in a closed row the reply's tables are split out the same way (`cmdBlock`). The list spans the pane and centres each item on the reading column; an item that holds a wide table is wider by the extra width and padded by half on each side, since Herdr clips an item on both axes. The extra width is in the part's measure key, so a resize measures the table again.
- **Collapsed reply**: with `truncate` off (the default) the whole reply and the whole prompt (line limits `NO_CUT`, no `PREVIEW_CHARS` cut), so no Show more anywhere; the open turn's YOU block is full (`full`, with Show less only after a Show all with `truncate` on, `less`) and LATEST UPDATE is Markdown from the start, whole up to `LATEST_MD` (12,000) characters with a "… N more characters not shown" note past that, and no Show all or Show less (`whole`). With `truncate` on, `mdPreview` in `ui.js` takes whole source lines of the reply that fit Reply lines at the reading width (a blank line counts half, a code line one; a paragraph longer than what is left is cut at a word with " …"; a cut fence is closed), and the row draws it with TextView Markdown at the reading size, selectable and with no link rules. Its height is measured in the probe as a part of its own (`cmd:<turn key>`); the row's height is the rest of the row (`mdBase`, image tiles included) plus that measure, and until then `mdEst`. While a search is on, the row keeps the flattened text around the match.
- **Highlight by drag**: in any turn, open or closed, a press and drag selects text, and only a press that does not move opens or closes anything (`selectGesture`, `DRAG_SLOP`). A closed row's reply start and the open turn's YOU block are selectable TextViews. A closed row's message lines (and a reply start that is not Markdown: a stop's reason, a search) are the shape's lines, so they are drawn by `selLines` as `TextView.html` (`htmlLine`, joined by the spaces the wrap took out and never by `<br>`, which Herdr 0.1.15 draws as nothing while it wraps the text itself; at most `MD_MAX` bytes a view, `htmlChunks`, memoised per line list), each block clipped to its rows and moved up 0.5 px, since TextView sets text half a px lower than a centred line. TextView draws in the theme's text colour whatever its parent or the HTML sets (neither `<font color>` nor a style attribute reaches it), so only lines in that colour are selectable; a quote line, an agent's message, a coloured bubble and a stop's reason stay plain lines, as do all lines while a search marks its hits. A mod cannot read a selection (Herdr issue 1110), so `q` quotes what `⌘C` copied when it comes from the reply or message of the selected turn, an open turn or a turn on screen (`copiedPart`, `screenTurns`), in Select view too, and otherwise the whole reply.
- **Day bands** (`dayItem`): 46 px, lines in `border` either side of a 24 px pill filled with `bandFill` (kept per theme), the day in `foreground` 12 px semibold, the date and count in `muted`. **Sticky day**: `stickyDay()` finds the day band above the list item at the top of the pane (`topKey`, set while the list draws) and the body draws the same pill 6 px from the top, over the list; not when a band, or the item right before one, is at the top. The drawing pass may not redraw, so a change sets `stickyDue` and a 120 ms timer redraws.
- **Turn list**, oldest at the top, newest at the bottom, opened scrolled to the newest turn. A short list sits at the bottom of the pane, by the newest turn. Each row is one exchange, drawn like a chat, 16 px in from each side with 8 px above and 10 px below. ## Which agent

What differs from Claude Code for each agent. [RECIPE.md](../RECIPE.md#how-it-behaves) describes Claude Code.

**Codex**: rollouts at `~/.codex/sessions/YYYY/MM/DD/rollout-<time>-<uuid>.jsonl`, read by `extract.sh <file> <offset> <chunk> codex` with `extract-codex.jq`. The session is the rollout the process has open (`lsof -p <pid>`, the root one when subagents' rollouts are open too), else the id in `codex resume <id>`; no file exists until the first prompt, so the pane waits. **OpenCode**: one SQLite database at `${XDG_DATA_HOME:-~/.local/share}/opencode/opencode.db`, read only by `sqlite3 -readonly` in `opencode.sh`, with a session's "file" written `<database>#<id>`. 1.x keeps sessions in `session`, `message` and `part` (`opencode-read.sql`). 2.x keeps them in `session_v2` and `session_message`, one row per message typed user, assistant, idle, system or compaction, where a reply belongs to the prompt before it by `seq` (`opencode-read-v2.sql`). 2.x copies 1.x sessions into its tables and stops writing the old ones, so both tables present means 2.x (1.19 already has an empty `session_message`). A read counts settled prompts instead of bytes, and returns the turns after the last finished one each time with `"rewind": true`, because OpenCode rewrites a running turn in place. The session is the one the terminal title `OC | <title>` fits (cut to `…` when long), else `-s <id>`, else the newest root session in the folder updated since the process started (on 2.x, where `/new` opens a tab and old tabs stay, the one whose turn began last, `time_busy`); with another OpenCode process in the folder that last step is a guess with Choose. 2.x terminals share one `opencode serve --service`, which is not counted as another OpenCode. A fresh process with no prompt yet waits, so it never takes an older session. **Pi**: one JSONL file per session at `${PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR:-~/.pi/agent}/sessions/--<cwd with / as ->--/<start time>_<id>.jsonl`, read by `extract.sh <file> <offset> <chunk> pi` with `extract-pi.jq`. The file is a tree (`id`/`parentId`) read in file order. Pi keeps no file open and runs under node with its process renamed `pi`, so the session is `--session <path|id>` from its command line, else the newest file in the folder that Pi wrote since the process started (pickOpenCode, with the file's mtime as when its last turn ran); with another `pi` in the folder that is a guess with Choose. Pi records no turn time, so `d` has no `ms` and the pane counts from the prompt. **Cursor**: one JSONL file per chat at `${CURSOR_DATA_DIR:-~/.cursor}/projects/<cwd with each run of non-alphanumerics as one -, trimmed>/agent-transcripts/<id>/<id>.jsonl`, read by `extract.sh <file> <offset> <chunk> cursor` with `extract-cursor.jq`. Lines are `{role, message: {content: [text | tool_use]}}` and `{type: "turn_ended", status: success|error|aborted}`. A prompt's text is `<timestamp>Thursday, Nov 5, 2026, 12:43 PM (UTC-6)</timestamp>` (the prompt's time, to the minute) and `<user_query>` (what was typed). Hidden thinking is `[REDACTED]`, alone or after the reply's last blank line, and is dropped; kept thinking is joined on with nothing to mark it, so it stays. An image is the text `[Image]`. There are no tokens, model or times, so the panel stamps a prompt and a turn end it sees arrive after the first full read with `at`, and `s.clock` (kept through a re-read) holds those per turn number. Cursor sometimes writes the whole file again (after `Esc`, keeping only the last `turn_ended`), so `extract.sh` also reads up to 4 KB before the offset and prints their cksum as `anchor`, with a closing `settle` naming the next one; a mismatch makes `replaced()` read the file again, and each prompt emits a `d` first so no turn stays open. The process (node running `cursor-agent/versions/<v>/index.js`) keeps `<store folder>/chats/<md5 of cwd>/<chat id>/store.db` open, so `locate.sh cursor-open` names the chat from that path alone (`lsof`, the newest store); the database is never read. The store folder is the data folder in older builds and `~/.config/cursor` in newer ones, while transcripts stay under the data folder, so `cursorRoots` searches the data folder first and the store's folder second. `/clear` opens no store until its first prompt. Else `--resume <id>`, else the folder's newest transcript since the process started (pickOpenCode), a guess with Choose when another Cursor runs there. **Antigravity**: the CLI (`agy`, a native program) writes one conversation to `${AGY_DATA_DIR:-~/.gemini/antigravity-cli}/brain/<id>/.system_generated/logs/transcript_full.jsonl` (`transcript.jsonl` beside it cuts long fields), read by `extract.sh <file> <offset> <chunk> antigravity` with `extract-agy.jq`. A line is a step `{step_index, source, type, status, created_at, content, input_tokens, cache_read_tokens, output_tokens, tool_calls: [{name, args}], error, thinking}`: `USER_INPUT` is a prompt (its words in `<USER_REQUEST>`, and a model change as "The user changed setting `Model Selection` from A to B (Level)", which becomes `ctx`), `PLANNER_RESPONSE` is the model's text, tool calls and tokens (`input_tokens` leaves out the cached ones), and the one with text and no tool call ends the turn; `GENERIC` steps are tool output and are dropped with `grep` before `jq`. agy holds the conversation's lock file open (`<data>/presence/<id>.lock`), so `locate.sh agy-open` names the conversation from `/proc/<pid>/fd` where there is one, else `lsof` (the pid and its children; of several, the folder with the newest transcript); else `--conversation <id>`; a fresh conversation writes its file with the first prompt, so the pane waits. **Amp** is known and not read (`UNREAD` and `unreadOf` in `harness.js`): it keeps its threads on its own servers, not on disk, and Threads reads only local files. An Amp terminal gets the "Amp isn't supported" page, with its mark, that reason in one line and a button that opens the README's "Why Amp isn't supported" section (`open <url>`). None of these agents reports status to Herdr here, so the pane looks again every 3 s (`POLL_MS`). The badge carries the agent's mark (`MARKS` in `harness.js`, the icons Herdr itself ships, see ICONS.md) and name, and toasts, banners and the legend use that name. OpenCode has no agent messages, because subagents are child sessions, and Pi has no subagents. A Codex, OpenCode or Pi turn with no usage shows no token count. A quote into OpenCode's folded paste (three lines or more, or over 150 characters) ends with a zero-width space, because OpenCode trims the blank line otherwise (`quotePaste`).
