Recommendation

Press p on the turn you are reading. It joins a short tray that sits just above the footer, and the text you are reading does not move. Press 1 to come back later, and you land at the paragraph where you stopped. Press p again to unpin, and u if you unpinned by mistake.

Up to five pins, kept per session in a small file, so they survive closing the panel, restarting Herdr and resuming the session.

How to read this page. Proposal is design only. Proven ran in a throwaway patched copy of the panel, in an isolated test window on the mini. Not tested could not be run. The frames are drawings. The images under "Proven or not" are the real panel.

The moment

You are partway through a long reply, and a newer reply needs you. Four steps, and only two keys. Click any image to see it full size. Arrow keys step through every image on the page, and Esc closes.

Your decisions

Everything else is settled below, with reasons. These four are yours.

1. Should the pins sit at the bottom or the top?

At the bottom they sit just above the footer. At the top they sit under the header, next to the pin button. I recommend the bottom. In the real panel, pinning at the top pushed the text you are reading down 35 pixels, and it moves again each time you add or remove a pin. At the bottom it stayed still. See Two layouts.

2. What should happen with a sixth pin?

I recommend refusing it and saying "Pins are full (5). Unpin one first." The other choice is to drop the oldest pin and offer undo. Refusing costs one extra step. Dropping can quietly lose the reply you most meant to come back to. The number five fits the keys 1 to 5 and takes about 140 pixels of height.

3. When you come back, how exact should the landing be?

Herdr does not let a mod read or set the scroll position to the pixel, only jump to an item in the list. Today a reply's text between code blocks is one item. So returning lands at the start of that whole block. In your real sessions, an estimated 44% of replies (325 of 734) have a block taller than a 500 pixel pane, so you could land more than a screen above where you stopped.

I recommend drawing long replies one paragraph at a time, which brings that down to an estimated 3% (25 of 734), and you land at the top of your paragraph. I tested this and it works. The cost is more build, and the spacing around headings and lists needs tuning, because it shifted in the test. Reply selection is not affected, since selecting already uses the separate Select view. The other choice is to land at the block and skip this work.

4. Is a second file on disk fine?

The docs promise that settings.json is the only file Threads writes. Pins need pins.json beside it, holding the session id, a turn fingerprint, the place you stopped and the first 80 characters of the prompt. It stays on your machine. I recommend yes, and I would update the docs. The other choice is Herdr's per-mod local storage, which saves no visible file. I have not proven it survives a restart or reaches a second pane, and pins.json did both.

Pin and unpin by keyboardp toggles the selected turn. 1 to 5 return. u undoes the last unpin.All are free in the panel's key table. If you set your quote key to p or u, quote keeps the letter and pin gives it up. The header button still works. Herdr owns chords, so a chord is not available.
Pin and unpin by mouseThe pin button in the header, which acts on the selected turn. The pin icon in the open card's head. The unpin icon on each tray row.You may be mid-reply with the card head scrolled away, so one control must always be on screen. Herdr's list rows cannot have their own buttons, and the panel can only tell where you clicked from left to right, not up and down. I tried a pin in the left margin of each row. It overlapped the selection ring, so I dropped it.
Finding out about itThe footer hint, the header tooltip ("Pin #7"), and the first toast: "Pinned #7 · press 1 to come back". Tray rows show their digit.The moment you pin is when you need the return key, so that is when it is taught.
Where pins liveA tray outside the list, between the list and footer. Rows are 26 px. It folds to one line of chips when rows would take over 30% of the pane.Being outside the list keeps it on screen while the list scrolls, and normal click and hover work there. The fold protects short panes.
Coming backClick the tray row or press its digit. The card opens at the place you stopped.The place is saved when you pin, and updated while you keep reading that turn. Still runningA spinner and "Working", using the panel's existing live state. "No reply yet" when there is no live state.Same words and colours as the list, so nothing new to learn.
With searchThe tray stays and is never filtered. With day bandsBands do not change. Tray rows show the time for today, then "Yesterday" or "4 Nov".A bare "5:54 AM" would mislead on an older pin.
Closing, restarting, resumingPins survive all three. They belong to one session.Stored by agent and session id. Another session's pins wait until you open it. A forked session gets a new id, so pins stay with the original. After /clear, a pane showing both sessions shows both sets. Two panes on one session share pins.
A turn that is goneThe row stays, dimmed, with its saved text and "Gone". Clicking says so. You remove it yourself.Removing it silently would look like a bug and lose your note of what it was. "Gone" only shows once the session is fully read, and "Reading…" shows before that.
Unpinning by accidentA toast says "Unpinned #7 · u to undo". u puts it back in the same place, with your reading place.No confirmation dialog, since unpinning should stay one action. Undo history lasts until the pane closes.

All five agents

Row numbers are not stable. Comparing an older build of the row model with the current one on your real Claude sessions, the old number still pointed at the same turn for only 21 of 529 rows. So a pin stores a fingerprint instead: the prompt's time, a hash of its text, and a count for repeats. That found 529 of 529. It needs no change to how any agent is read. No agent's records make a pin unstable in what I tested.
